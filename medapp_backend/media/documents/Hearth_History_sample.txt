### **Chief Complaint**  
Chest pain radiating to the left arm, associated with diaphoresis and dyspnea, leading to a diagnosis of ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI).  

---

### **History of Present Illness**  
Mr. Smith, a 58-year-old male with a history of hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and hyperlipidemia, presented to the emergency department on October 20, 2023, reporting sudden-onset substernal chest pain rated 9/10 in intensity. The pain began while he was watching television and radiated to his left arm and jaw, accompanied by nausea, diaphoresis, and shortness of breath. Symptoms persisted for 90 minutes prior to arrival.  

ECG revealed ST-segment elevation in anterior leads (V2–V4), confirming an anterior STEMI. Emergent cardiac catheterization showed 95% occlusion of the proximal left anterior descending (LAD) artery, successfully treated with a drug-eluting stent. Troponin-I peaked at 25 ng/mL (normal <0.04 ng/mL). Post-procedure, he was stabilized in the cardiac care unit (CCU) and transitioned to telemetry.
